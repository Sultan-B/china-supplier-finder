Our Supplier Research Methodology

We help international small brands, importers and wholesalers organize an initial search for potential Chinese suppliers. Our work is public-source desk research: evidence is recorded, gaps are made visible, and conclusions are kept within what the sources support.

1. Research workflow

  1. Define the brief. Record product, must-have specifications, target quantity, destination market, preferred supplier type and any required standards.
  2. Discover candidates. Search accessible official company websites, public business directories, trade-fair exhibitor pages and other relevant public sources.
  3. Review public evidence. Capture product descriptions, company-stated capabilities, location and public business contact details. Record the source and date for each material claim.
  4. Assess product fit. Compare available public product information against the buyer's stated requirements. Mark unconfirmed details as gaps.
  5. Classify supplier-type evidence. Apply cautious evidence labels rather than claiming verified factory status.
  6. Score and report. Apply the five-dimension research match rubric, add limitations and propose questions for the buyer to ask.

2. Supplier-type evidence labels

LabelMeaningWhat it does not mean
Manufacturer evidence foundPublic sources show relevant manufacturing-related evidence, such as factory/capability descriptions, production-process information or company statements about manufacturing.Not proof of ownership, actual production capacity or that the specific product is made at that site.
Trading-company indicatorsPublic descriptions emphasize sourcing, export, multi-brand product aggregation or trading services.Not proof that the company has no manufacturing operation.
Mixed business indicatorsPublic information indicates both manufacturing-related and trading/export activities.Not a legal classification or verification.
Insufficient public evidenceAvailable public information does not support a reasonable supplier-type observation.Not proof that the company is unsuitable or illegitimate.

3. Five-dimension research match score

Each dimension receives a score from 0 to 5. The total is out of 25. The score describes the strength and relevance of public information found for this specific sourcing brief; it is not an overall company rating.

DimensionWhat is assessed
Product MatchHow closely public product information matches the buyer's product and use case.
Public Information CompletenessWhether useful company, product, location and business information is publicly available.
Manufacturer-related EvidenceHow much relevant public evidence exists about manufacturing-related activity. This is not factory verification.
Public Contact AvailabilityWhether a usable public business contact channel is available from a traceable source.
Specification EvidenceWhether public material addresses the buyer's key materials, dimensions, standards, customization or other required specifications.

Score anchors used for each dimension

0No usable public evidence found.
1Very limited evidence; major information gaps.
2Partial evidence; important details remain unconfirmed.
3Reasonable relevant evidence, with some gaps.
4Strong, directly relevant public evidence.
5Detailed and directly relevant public evidence for this dimension.

A high score does not prove factory ownership, product quality, capacity, compliance, reliability or transaction safety. A low score may reflect limited public disclosure rather than poor business quality. Scores are tied to the research date and the buyer's specific brief.

4. Sources and traceability

  • We prefer official company sources for company-stated products, addresses and contacts.
  • We may use public directories, trade-show listings and other accessible sources as supporting context.
  • We record source URLs and the date reviewed. When possible, a source is linked to the specific information it supports.
  • We distinguish company claims from independently corroborated public information.
  • We do not fabricate missing details. Unknown information is marked as unavailable or requiring confirmation.

5. Questions buyers should ask suppliers

  • Do you manufacture this product at your own facility, or do you source it from another factory?
  • What is your minimum order quantity for the requested specification?
  • Can you provide current product specifications, material details and test reports?
  • Which certifications apply to this exact product and destination market, and can you provide documents for independent verification?
  • Can you provide samples, customization options, production lead time and a written quotation?
  • Can you provide the registered company name and the legal entity that will appear on the contract and invoice?

6. Scope and limitations

China Supplier Finder provides public-source desk research only. We do not conduct factory visits, audits, business-license authentication, ownership checks, sanctions screening, certificate validation, laboratory tests, product inspections, supplier outreach, price negotiation or transaction management unless a separate written scope is agreed and the service is actually available.

Information may be incomplete, outdated, inaccurate or supplied by the company itself. Buyers should independently verify legal identity, factory status, certifications, product quality, capacity, payment details and contractual terms before placing orders or transferring funds.

This methodology is a working research standard and may be refined as the service develops. It is not legal, compliance, quality assurance or investment advice.
