CANDIDATE 01

HAERS

Yongkang, Zhejiang · Company-stated drinkware manufacturer

Official website
Company-stated manufacturingOEM / ODM describedNot independently verified

Company and product profile

HAERS' official drinkware website describes the company as a manufacturer of insulated drinkware and lists stainless steel bottles, vacuum tumblers and sports bottles. Its company profile describes OEM and ODM services, including customization and ready-model options. Its company history page identifies a Yongkang production base and describes in-house insulated-bottle manufacturing milestones.

Evidence interpretation: The official pages provide direct company-stated evidence of stainless-steel drinkware products and OEM/ODM services. They do not independently establish the contracting entity for a future order, the exact factory assigned to a particular SKU, or the performance of that SKU.

Product-fit assessment

  • Category fit: Direct — stainless steel bottles and insulated tumblers are explicitly listed.
  • Customization fit: OEM/ODM and customization options are described by the company.
  • Specification fit: Not determined; no buyer-specific capacity, dimensions, steel grade, lid design, insulation test, packaging or target price was supplied.
  • Order fit: MOQ, sample cost, lead time and order-specific capacity require a quote for the exact model.

Supplier-type evidence

Classification: Manufacturer evidence found — company-stated. The official site describes manufacturing operations and production bases. This is not a factory audit, ownership check or independent production-capacity confirmation.

Public contact and information gaps

Contact route: Use the contact route on the official HAERS website. A direct sales email or named contact was not captured in the sources used for this sample.

Not established: Buyer-specific MOQ and price; exact production site for the quoted SKU; current available capacity; sample and mass-production lead times; current test reports for the intended product and destination market; contractual entity and payment details.

Questions for the buyer to ask

  1. Which legal entity will sign the contract and issue the invoice, and which factory will manufacture this exact SKU?
  2. Can you provide a current catalogue and technical drawing, including capacity, dimensions and net weight?
  3. What are the inner and outer material grades, and can you provide material declarations and product-specific test reports?
  4. What are the MOQ, sample fee, tooling fee, unit-price tiers and Incoterms for the requested customization?
  5. Can you provide recent production evidence or a live video walkthrough tied to the proposed production site and model?

OUR METHODOLOGY

Evidence first. Clear limitations. Useful next steps.

Every report distinguishes what a public source says from what we can reasonably infer—and what still needs to be checked by the buyer.

Source traceability

We record source URLs, access dates and the specific facts each source supports. Official company websites are preferred for company-stated product and contact details; independent public sources may provide additional context.

Supplier-type evidence

We use cautious labels: manufacturer evidence found, trading-company indicators, mixed business indicators, or insufficient public evidence. These are research observations—not verified legal classifications.

Product-fit assessment

We compare public product information against the buyer's stated specifications. Missing or unconfirmed requirements are marked as questions, not assumed to be satisfied.

Illustrative 25-point research match score

Five dimensions are scored from 0 to 5: product match, public information completeness, manufacturer-related evidence, public contact availability and specification evidence. The score summarizes relevant public evidence found; it is not a credit rating, quality certification, risk guarantee or probability of successful sourcing.

0 — No usable public evidence found
1 — Very limited evidence
2 — Partial evidence; important gaps
3 — Reasonable public evidence; some gaps
4 — Strong public evidence for this dimension
5 — Detailed, directly relevant public evidence

Scores reflect information reviewed at the research date. A high score does not prove factory ownership, product quality, capacity, compliance or reliability. A low score may reflect limited public disclosure rather than poor business quality.
